**I'm <a href="https://fitbod.me/">Fitbod</a>'s Head of Data Science, Research &amp; AI.** Fitbod creates strength-training workouts that adapt to your body, recovery, and progress. I joined the team in 2021, after being a passionate Fitbod user for a few months.

At Fitbod **I'm responsible for the algorithm that generates each workout**, for analytics and A/B testing, for applied research, for patent applications, for hiring and developing data scientists, and for ensuring other teams get correct answers when they ask Claude and Codex data questions. I also participate in Fitbod's overall business planning and help build the product roadmap.

Some things I've done at Fitbod:

- **Set up Fitbod’s A/B testing framework**

  I set up Fitbod's A/B testing methodology and pipeline, enforced the same standards across all squads, and worked with my team and Product to choose appropriate KPIs and guardrail metrics. We went from 4 A/B tests in 2021 to 57 in the first half of 2026.

- **Sped up algo development by 9x**

  When I joined Fitbod the algorithm that generates the workouts ran on the client. Any algo changes had to go through the client teams, which slowed down development. I rewrote the entire algo in Python and migrated it server-side. That put the algo under my direct responsibility and enabled us to ship 9x more algo A/B tests per year.

  As a bonus the iOS and Android algo are now the same (there were discrepancies before), we get to update it without users having to re-install the app, and server-side logging has made it much easier to reproduce algo output and investigate user complaints.

- **Increased exercise compliance by 42%**

  With the algo running server-side, my team and I were able to leverage our 10 years of workout data to personalize the workouts more. As a result, we've reduced the rate of replaced and skipped exercises by 42%.

- **Increased sets/reps/weights accuracy by 46%**

  By leveraging our 10 years of workout data, my team and I have made our capability estimation much better: users went from following our exact sets/reps/weights recommendations 30% of the time to doing it 44% of the time.

- **Sped up strength gains by 4x**

  In 2021 the average Fitbod user could bench press 6% more weight after doing the exercise ten times. In 2026 that's 26%. That's a direct result of more personalized workouts.

- **Partnered with leading sports scientists**

  I reached out to professors [Brad Schoenfeld](https://www.lehman.edu/academics/health-human-services-nursing/exercise-sciences-recreation/bradley-schoenfeld/) and [Andy Galpin](https://www.parker.edu/research-at-parker/research-staff/andy-galpin/) and pitched a research collaboration. They said yes, brought along Dr. [Federica Conti](https://www.parker.edu/research-at-parker/research-staff/federica-conti/), and the four of us just co-authored a research paper that looks into the determinants of long-term strength training adherence - upcoming in [Frontiers in Sports and Active Living](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2026.1855668/abstract).

- **Created a better 1-Rep-Max formula**

  Your 1-Rep-Max is the maximum weight you can lift for a given exercise in one set of one rep. It's mostly a theoretical number, estimated from whatever sets/reps/weights you've actually lifted for that exercise.

  1-Rep-Max estimation is noisy, based on formulas derived from small samples and only a few exercises. I mined Fitbod's data - 303,494 near-failure sets from 14,966 users across 388 exercises spanning 16 muscle groups - to create a better formula. Pre-print [here](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17495), currently under review.
